# How to play BANG!

## Starting a game

- **Solo**: in the lobby choose the number of players (4–7, or 8 with Dodge City), tick the expansions you want, choose the **computer difficulty** (Easy, Normal or Hard) and press "Start game". Computer players fill the other seats. Seats are shuffled every game.
- **With friends**: press "Create room" and send the invite link. Friends open the link, enter a name and join, from any network. Computers fill empty seats. If someone disconnects, the game waits 15 seconds and then lets the computer play for them until they come back.
- In an online room the host can also set the computer difficulty. You can chat by text or switch on voice.
- **Language**: the language menu at the top of the lobby and the game switches between 中文 (Chinese), English and Tiếng Việt (Vietnamese) at any time. The choice is remembered on this device, and in online games everyone can use their own language.

## Roles and goals

Everyone is dealt a secret role; only the **Sheriff** is revealed.

| Role | Goal |
|---|---|
| Sheriff | Eliminate every Outlaw and the Renegade |
| Deputy | Protect the Sheriff and eliminate every Outlaw and the Renegade |
| Outlaw | Kill the Sheriff |
| Renegade | Be the last one standing (finally beating the Sheriff one-on-one) |

| Players | Roles |
|---|---|
| 4 | Sheriff, Renegade, 2 Outlaws |
| 5 | Sheriff, Deputy, Renegade, 2 Outlaws |
| 6 | Sheriff, Deputy, Renegade, 3 Outlaws |
| 7 | Sheriff, 2 Deputies, Renegade, 3 Outlaws |
| 8 (Dodge City) | Sheriff, 2 Deputies, 2 Renegades, 3 Outlaws |

The game opens with a **character screen**: every character available in this game (depending on the expansions you ticked) is laid out with its life and ability. Everyone picks at the same time; characters someone has picked glow and show that player's name.

- If two players pick the same character, each tosses one coin and heads wins (both heads or both tails: toss again).
- If three or more clash, each tosses 3 coins and the most heads wins; tied players toss again.
- Whoever misses out picks again from the characters that are left.

Life depends on the character (most have 4, some 3, Big Spencer 9, Gary Looter 5) and the Sheriff gets +1. You start with as many cards as your life.

## A turn

1. **Draw**: draw 2 cards (some characters differ). If you have Dynamite or Jail in front of you, you draw! for it first.
2. **Play**: play as many cards as you like, but only one BANG! per turn (except with a Volcanic or Willy the Kid). You may have only one copy of each card in play, and only one weapon.
3. **Discard**: you may not keep more cards than your current life. Press "End turn".

## Distance and range

- Neighbours are at distance 1, the next ones at distance 2, and so on (counting whichever way round is shorter, skipping the dead).
- BANG! can only hit someone within range. With no weapon your range is 1; your weapon sets your range.
- A Mustang makes others see you 1 further away; a Scope makes you see others 1 closer.
- When the "Dist" on someone's plate is red, they are within your range.

## Card reference

| Card | Effect |
|---|---|
| BANG! | Shoot someone in range; they must play a Missed! or lose 1 life |
| Missed! | Play when shot to dodge the shot |
| Beer | Regain 1 life; can be drunk when dying. No effect with only two players left |
| Saloon | Everyone regains 1 life |
| Stagecoach / Wells Fargo | Draw 2 / draw 3 |
| General Store | Reveal one card per living player; starting with you, everyone takes one |
| Gatling | Shoot every other player once |
| Indians! | Every other player discards a BANG! or loses 1 life |
| Duel | You and a player take turns discarding BANG!; the first who cannot loses 1 life |
| Panic! | Take one card from a player at distance 1 |
| Cat Balou | Make any player discard one card |
| Barrel | When shot, draw!: a Heart counts as a Missed! |
| Dynamite | At the start of your turn draw!: a Spade 2–9 explodes for 3 damage, otherwise it passes on |
| Jail | Place in front of anyone but the Sheriff; at the start of their turn they draw!, and unless it is a Heart they skip the turn |
| Mustang / Scope | Others see you at +1 / you see others at −1 |
| Weapons | Volcanic (range 1, unlimited BANG!), Schofield 2, Remington 3, Rev. Carabine 4, Winchester 5 |

**Draw!**: flip the top card of the deck and look at its suit and value (top-left corner).

## Expansions

### Dodge City

40 more cards and 15 characters; allows 8 players.

- **Green cards** are put into play first and can be used **from your next turn** (discard them to use them). A green card with a dashed outline on a name plate cannot be used yet.
  - Bible, Ten Gallon Hat, Iron Plate, Sombrero: count as a Missed! when you are shot (the Bible also draws 1).
  - Knife and Derringer hit distance 1; Pepperbox hits anyone in range; Buffalo Rifle hits anyone.
  - Canteen regains 1 life, Can Can is a Cat Balou, Conestoga is a Panic! at any distance, Howitzer is a Gatling, Pony Express draws 3.
- **Cards that need one more discard**: Rag Time (take a card at any distance), Brawl (everyone discards a card you choose), Springfield (shoot at any distance), Tequila (anyone regains 1 life), Whisky (you regain 2 life).
- Punch hits distance 1 without counting as your BANG!. Dodge counts as a Missed! and draws 1. Binocular works like a Scope, Hideout like a Mustang.

### High Noon, A Fistful of Cards and Wild West Show (events)

From the Sheriff's second turn, an event card is revealed at the start of each of the Sheriff's turns and lasts until the next one. The current event is shown at the bottom left and lies on the table. The last card of the event deck is a finale (High Noon: everyone loses 1 life at the start of their turn; A Fistful of Cards: you are shot once for each card in your hand).

### Gold Rush

- You gain 1 **gold nugget** for each life you make another player lose. You can also trade a Beer from your hand for 1 nugget.
- During your play phase a **shop** appears below (3 face-up equipment cards):
  - Brown-bordered items are used straight away (Shot, Bottle, Pardner, Gold Rush, Rum, Union Pacific).
  - Black-bordered items stay in front of you (Horseshoe, Pickaxe, Boots, Lucky Charm, Rucksack, Gun Belt, Calumet, Gold Pan, and Wanted, which you put on someone else).
- Pay "price + 1" nuggets to discard someone's black-bordered item; Panic! and Cat Balou cannot touch them.

### The Valley of Shadows

15 more cards and 8 characters: Aim (a hit costs 2 life), Fanning (shoot two neighbouring players), Tomahawk (distance 2), Backfire (dodge and shoot back), Escape (avoid a brown card), Bandidos, Poker, Tornado, Last Call; equipment Lemat (any card as BANG!), Shotgun, Rattlesnake and Bounty; and Ghost, which brings a dead player back as a ghost.

### Wild West Show

8 new characters and an event deck (Bone Orchard, Darling Valentine, Helena Zontero, Miss Susanna, Showdown, Sacagaway). The finale, Wild West Show, changes everyone's goal to "be the last one standing".

## Death, rewards and penalties

- When your life reaches 0 you can drink a Beer at once to survive; otherwise you die and your role is revealed.
- Whoever kills an Outlaw draws 3 cards as a reward.
- If the Sheriff kills a Deputy, the Sheriff discards every card in hand and in play.

## Reading the screen

The game starts in **first-person view**: you sit in your seat, see your own hand and drag the screen to turn your head. "Overhead view" at the bottom right shows the whole table from above.

- The number of cards each figure holds is their hand size.
- Name plates show life (bullets), distance and equipment. Tap the character name on a plate (or "Abilities" at the top) to see everyone's ability, life and what each piece of equipment does.
- Equipment lies on the table in front of its owner with a small prop (pistol, rifle, spyglass, barrel, horseshoe, dynamite…) and a label everyone can read; weapons show their range.
- A jailed player has an iron cage drop over them; if it is you, bars come down across your screen.
- When someone shoots, they draw a revolver, swing out the cylinder, load the rounds one by one, close it, cock the hammer and fire (each step has a sound). Rapid fire such as a Gatling does not reload in between. A dodge pops up "Missed!" and a hit flashes red. When you shoot, you see your own hands loading in first-person; when you are hit, the edges of your screen flash red.
- "Sound on / off" at the top toggles the sound effects.
- At the end, the results screen lets you read the **full game log** or look at the final table, then reopen the results.

## Controls

- **Playing a card**: tap a card in your hand; the possible targets appear below, tap one to play. Panic! and Cat Balou also ask which of their cards; cards with a cost ask you to tap the extra card to discard. On a phone you can double-tap a card.
- **Green cards in play**: on your turn a "Green cards in play" button appears below; tap it, then choose a target.
- **Character abilities**: abilities you activate yourself (Sid Ketchum, Chuck Wengam, José Delgado, Doc Holyday and others) appear as buttons below.
- **When shot**: tap a highlighted Missed! to dodge; with a Barrel a "Draw! with Barrel" button appears.
- Online, every decision has a time limit; when it runs out the computer decides for you.

## How the computer guesses roles

Computer players **never peek**. They get exactly the information you see on your screen and can only judge sides by what everyone can see happen. They do not pick a target just because of a score: like a person, they first work out what each player **might** be, then consider how sure they are, whether that player has shot at them and whether they are about to die, and only then decide whether to shoot.

### What the computer knows

- Its own role.
- Who the Sheriff is (public anyway).
- The roles of dead players (revealed on death, also for anyone brought back by a Ghost).
- How many of each role there are (for example with 7 players: 1 Sheriff, 2 Deputies, 3 Outlaws, 1 Renegade), so it can work out how many Outlaws are still hidden, but not who they are.

It does not know the roles of other living players or anyone else's hand (unless the Sacagaway event reveals hands, when everyone can see them).

### Step 1: record who did what to whom

Every time someone **targets a specific player**, a public "interaction" is recorded. Positive means hostile, negative means helpful, and the size says how clear the action is:

| Action | Weight |
| --- | --- |
| BANG!, Duel, Punch, Springfield, Knife, Derringer, Pepperbox, Buffalo Rifle, Tomahawk, the double BANG! of the Sniper event, Black Flower's Club as BANG!, shots from abilities such as Doc Holyday, Jacky Murieta or Evelyn Shebang | +1 |
| Fanning (two targets): each target | +1 |
| Aim (a hit costs 2 life) | +1.3 |
| Jail | +0.8 |
| Rattlesnake | +0.7 |
| Bounty, Wanted | +0.6 |
| Panic!, Cat Balou, Conestoga, Rag Time, shooting a card in play with BANG!, paying nuggets to discard someone's equipment | +0.6 |
| Pat Brennan taking a card in play | +0.5 |
| Backfire (only when shot first) | +0.5 |
| Jesse Jones drawing from someone's hand, Flint Westwood swapping cards | +0.3 |
| Henry Block's automatic return shot | +0.3 |
| The card picked from each player in a Brawl (everyone has to discard) | +0.2 |
| **Gatling, Howitzer, Indians! (hit everyone)**: each player | +0.15 |
| The same, but that player has only 1 life left (you might kill them) | +0.6 |
| Killing a player (added on top) | +1.5 |
| Taking or discarding someone's **Jail** = freeing them | −0.6 |
| Taking or discarding someone's **Dynamite** = helping them | −0.3 |
| Buying someone a drink (Tequila, Shot), Blood Brothers | −1 |
| Bringing a dead player back with a Ghost | −1 |

**Actions that do not count at all** (no chosen target, or not a free choice): Bandidos, Poker, Tornado, Saloon, General Store, drinking your own Beer, Dynamite passing on by itself, event effects, draw!, Youl Grinner forcing others to give cards, Vulture Sam collecting a dead player's cards, and drawing abilities.

### Step 2: each computer estimates everyone's role (as probabilities)

Each computer player estimates, for every player whose role it cannot see, the chance that they are a **Deputy, Outlaw or Renegade**.

1. **It starts from the numbers.** For example, in a 7-player game where I am an Outlaw, the other 5 hidden players include 2 Deputies, 2 Outlaws and 1 Renegade, so each of them starts at "Deputy 40%, Outlaw 40%, Renegade 20%".
2. **Every action is a clue.** The computer asks: "If they were a Deputy (or an Outlaw, or the Renegade), how likely is it that they would do this?" The more likely, the more that role's probability goes up.

   | What they did | Looks like a Deputy | Looks like an Outlaw | Looks like the Renegade |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Shot the Sheriff | almost never (3%) | very often (100%) | sometimes (25%) |
   | Shot someone who looks like an Outlaw | very often (100%) | rarely (20%) | often (90%) |
   | Shot someone who looks like a Deputy | rarely (25%) | often (85%) | half the time (55%) |
   | Helped the Sheriff (a drink, freeing from Jail) | very often | almost never | often |
   | Helped someone who looks like an Outlaw | rarely | very often | sometimes |

   Heavier actions count more; an area attack such as a Gatling only counts 0.15 per player and barely changes anything.
3. **Who shot them is a clue too.** Being shot by the Sheriff means the Sheriff suspects them, so they look more like an Outlaw (only a weak clue, because the Sheriff can be wrong). Being shot by someone who looks like an Outlaw makes them look more like a good guy. These clues count half.
4. **The computer knows its own role.** So "who shot me" is also a clue: if I am a Deputy and someone keeps shooting me, they probably are an Outlaw.
5. **The numbers must add up.** If only 2 Outlaws are still hidden, four players cannot all look like Outlaws; the probabilities are adjusted together to fit the role counts.
6. **When someone dies and their role is revealed, everything is recalculated.** Anyone who shot them earlier is re-judged: if you kept shooting someone who turns out to be an Outlaw, you look very much like a Deputy.

**When nobody has done anything**, everyone stays at the starting odds, nobody looks especially suspicious, and the computer does not shoot just because of a "score" (see the threshold in the next step).

### A worked example

A 7-player game where seat 1 is the Sheriff. These numbers were calculated by the game itself (Hard difficulty):

| What happened | Whose point of view | Guess about whom | Sure they are an enemy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Outlaw A | everyone: Deputy 40%, Outlaw 40%, Renegade 20% | 39% |
| Outlaw B shoots the Sheriff | Outlaw A | B: Outlaw 81% | −12% (treated as a partner, never shot) |
| Outlaw B shoots the Sheriff | Deputy C | B: Outlaw 89% | 93% |
| Deputy C shoots B | Outlaw A | C: Deputy 62% | 72% |
| Deputy C shoots B | the other Deputy D | C: Deputy 39% (from a start of 20%) | 39% |
| The Sheriff shoots Outlaw E | Deputy C | E: Outlaw 60% | 67% |
| Outlaw A plays a Gatling | Deputy C | A: Outlaw 52% (barely up from the start) | 61% |

So once Outlaw B has shot the Sheriff, **the other Outlaws treat B as a partner** instead of shooting B because of a low score.

### Step 3: whether to shoot

1. **Turn the probabilities into "how sure am I that this is an enemy"**, differently for each role:
   - **Sheriff and Deputies**: chance of Outlaw + chance of Renegade × 0.35 (once the Outlaws are all dead, the Renegade becomes enemy number one).
   - **Outlaws**: the Sheriff is always the first target; for anyone else, chance of Deputy + chance of Renegade × 0.55 − chance of Outlaw × 0.3, so the more someone looks like an Outlaw, the less they get shot.
   - **Renegade**: early on mainly shoots Outlaws, then Deputies; **protects the Sheriff once the Sheriff is down to 2 life or less**, and only turns on the Sheriff when the two of them are left; once the Outlaws are dead, everyone is a target.
   - **Wild West Show finale**: everyone must be the last one standing, so everyone is an enemy.
2. **No evidence, no shooting.** The certainty must pass a threshold (Sheriff 40%, Deputy 50%, Outlaw 50%, Renegade 45%) and also be clearly above the starting odds for a player who has shown nothing (Sheriff +2%, Deputy +8%, Outlaw +10%, Renegade +4%). So a Deputy will not shoot someone just because there are many Outlaws. As the game drags on the thresholds slowly drop (by up to 30%) to avoid stalemates.
3. **Human considerations:**
   - **Grudges**: someone who has shot me becomes a bit more tempting (unless I am sure they are on my side).
   - **Finishing off**: an enemy on 1 life is more tempting.
   - **Outlaws and the Sheriff**: if the Sheriff is in range, shoot the Sheriff.
4. **A little randomness.** Every decision has a small random element so the computer does not always do exactly the same thing; the easier the level, the more random.
5. **The Sheriff probes.** When the Sheriff has no clear target, there is a 25% chance of a probing shot at the most suspicious player (Outlaw chance at least 45%).
6. **Who counts as a friend.** Anyone the computer is less than 25% sure is an enemy (20% for Outlaws) is treated as a friend: it will buy them a drink, free them from Jail or remove their Dynamite, and bring them back with a Ghost.

### Difficulty

| | Easy | Normal | Hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reasoning | Only "who shot the Sheriff, who shot me"; no chains of reasoning, no counting roles | Full | Full |
| Randomness | High | Medium | Low |
| Grudges | Medium | Stronger | Light |
| Finishing off | Hardly | Yes | Keenly |
| Slips | 30% of turns go wrong, half of them ending the turn without playing | Occasionally | Never |

Over hundreds of computer-only games, Outlaws shooting fellow Outlaws dropped from 8% in the old version to about 3%, and Outlaws aim about 70% of their attacks at the Sheriff. On Normal the Outlaws win about half the games, the Sheriff's side a little over a third and the Renegade about one in eight; on Hard the Sheriff's side is a little stronger.

### Area attacks and other cards

- Gatling, Indians!, Brawl, Bandidos, Poker: the computer only plays these when **there are at least as many enemies as friends** at the table, to avoid hurting its own side too much.
- Jail, Rattlesnake, Bounty, Duel, Panic!, Cat Balou: only used on players it wants to attack. If there is no such target, Panic! and Cat Balou are used to **free a friend from Jail or remove their Dynamite**.
- Saloon: only played when the computer or a friend is hurt.

### Where it gets things wrong (just like people)

- A Deputy who never attacks anyone stays at the starting odds, so the Outlaws cannot tell who they are.
- The Renegade shoots Outlaws early on and looks like a Deputy, so the Outlaws may shoot them as one and the Sheriff may be fooled.
- When out of range, the computer can only shoot whoever it can reach, which is not always who it most wants.
- A probing shot by the Sheriff may hit a Deputy, who is then suspected for a while.
